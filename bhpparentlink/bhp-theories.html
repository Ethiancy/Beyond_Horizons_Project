Mysteries, Theories, and Hypothesis

In the History page, I stated that white holes and womrholes were likely not possible, and based on conventional consensus, that is the correct opinion. I don't think I explained adequately what a white hole was however, nor wormholes. To start off, some scientists stated that since black holes seem to just take and take and take matter as well as energy that it must go somewheres. White holes were the answer, a sort of exit for all of the matter and energy that a black hole takes in. I called it fantastical because white holes have been equated to what are essentially wormholes. They were be a physical exit for all that matter and energy, if a black hole is a drain then some scientists stated there must be faucet for that drain. In the respect that information could not be destroyed, it makes sense.

Wormholes are also similarly fantastical, points at which one may travel with specialized equipment or spacecraft, Interstellar immediately coming to mind. We do know that space is a physical quality of the universe in that black holes can physically bend and bring space itself with their angular momentum. Who's to say that space can't bend back onto itself and make a connection? It is theoretically possible, but such a thing is untested. Many don't consider wormholes beyond their function in instant space travel or portals to some sort of hell-space. Let us consider for a moment what sort of connection would have to be made, we know that such a connection could not be maintained by matter alone, nor energy. Keeping a connection between two points in space connected and essentially zipped together would require a truly immense amount of energy beyond all our currect energy capabilities and very likely incredible computing power to maintain such a connection. In this way, we can see how they are fantastical, albeit still theoretically possible option for travel.

Another similarly fantastical but not so infeasible possibility can be found in the past, quasi-stars. Like normal stars, quasi-stars would be powered by hydrogen, but only partially. Much more of the energy produced would be that of accretion, the same sort of energy produced by our own Sagittarius A but on a truly gargantuan scale. At the center of a quasi-star would be a black hole, and the material would fall into at from every side, essentially force-feeding the black hole at the center of it. As such, the black holes would become similarly gargantuan, possibly solving the current mystery that is supermassive and ultramassive black holes as they currently exist. Quasi-stars would however only be possibly in the very early universe, a few hundred million years after the big bang. As a result, all quasi-stars cannot form today, and very likely will never exist again unless the universe goes through expansions and contractions, that is; unless our universe will go through a big crunch and subsequently another big bang.
