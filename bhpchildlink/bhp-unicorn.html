The Unicorn

The smallest known black holes currently range from 5-10 M☉, with some smaller black hole candidates between 3-5 M☉. Unicorn, or the companion star/black hole to V723 Moncerotis, is one such candidate. What is known about V723 as a system is the apparent ellipsoidal nature of V723's main star, and the apparent lower luminosity of the companion object. Both objects produce light, hinting at either a star binary or a mass-transfer black hole-star binary, but regardless of which type there is a conspicious mass-tranfer occuring because of the low mass of the V723 main star. This in combination with the football shape of the V723 main has lead some to believe V723 main's sibling is probably a black hole. At about 1,500 lightyears, it would be the closest known black hole just a little closer than Gaia BH1, and it's very low size would also make it the smallest known black hole, however it is hard to definitively say whether or not it is a black hole due to how faint the system appears. Regardless, the interesting aspects of V723 Mon make it a good candidate for further research into black holes. Knowing where the cut-off is for neutron stars to black holes would lend scientists greater insight into universal laws.
